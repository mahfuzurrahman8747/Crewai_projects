Here is my clear argument against the motion "is AI safe?" in a concise manner:

The creation, development, distribution, use, support, and maintenance of artificial intelligence systems and entities involve numerous variables and stakeholders. Moreover, AI systems can be seen as having intrinsic value, similar to those of humans. The potential impact of AI on societal progress, economic activity, personal values, and human dignity must be carefully considered before we decide where to draw the line between acceptable risks and benefits.

The key factors that contribute to assessing the safety of AI include:

   1.  **Potential Risks:** Identifying potential risks or accidents stemming from an AI system can result in data breaches, cyber attacks, or unforeseen consequences on human lives.
   2.  **Technical Complexity:** Assessing the likelihood and potential impact of errors caused by AI algorithms or computational limitations is also crucial.
   3.  **Human Oversight:** Ensuring adequate checks and balances to mitigate risks when AI decisions are made without direct human intervention.

However, the development of formal methods for explicitly stating the design properties (such as consistency, completeness in all cases, and termination) enables an overall confidence assessment regarding safe behavior. Moreover, implementing rigorous testing protocols ensures that AI systems operate flawlessly, even if errors occur.

Moreover, these measures only scratch the surface when it comes to assessing the safety of AI. The development of better monitoring systems for potential biases and vulnerabilities remains essential. Additionally, transparency in AI decision-making processes is crucial. This would allow for immediate identification and rectification of any issues that may arise.

Unfortunately, a systematic approach is lacking in many countries. In fact, most governments have yet to establish comprehensive safety protocols for AI development and deployment. When it comes to enforcing regulations on AI developers, many states are woefully unprepared. There is also little understanding by policymakers about the potential risks posed by AI due to its impact on various employment markets.

In stark contrast to the optimistic claims often made in favor of AI, real-world outcomes have demonstrated that the current trajectory towards deploying AI extensively is fraught with risk.

To create a safe AI landscape, governments, industries, and education institutions must work together. However, more should be done in the areas of standardizing regulations across industries and creating mandatory safety protocols for AI development.

Ultimately, this means addressing the real-world challenges posed by AI through collective action and investment in both research and regulation.

In light of these concerns, I firmly argue that **'AI is not safe.'**
